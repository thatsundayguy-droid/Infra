Suez Traffic Claws Back Toward Pre-Crisis Levels as Carriers Return — Sokhna Positioned to Benefit

Suez Canal revenues hit $4.67bn in FY2025/26, up 23% year-on-year, as major carriers cautiously restore Red Sea routings nearly three years after the Houthi-driven disruption began. CMA CGM alone moved 199 vessels carrying 25.2 million tonnes through the canal in 2026 to date — already surpassing its full-year 2025 tonnage by roughly 34% — following an August 18 meeting between its leadership and the Suez Canal Authority. Maersk's CEO put the broader recovery at "about one-third" of normal Red Sea traffic, spread across four of thirteen services. Receipts remain roughly half the pre-disruption FY2022/23 record of $9.4bn, underlining how much runway remains.

For the I&C book, the read-through is direct: DP World Sokhna sits at the mouth of this recovery. The terminal closed 2025 at a record 1 million TEU against 1.75 million TEU of combined basin capacity, and a fuller Suez recovery — plus SCZone's parallel push to court US and Asian logistics investment (117 new projects, $7.3bn committed in FY2025/26, 73,500 jobs pipeline) — points toward continued utilisation gains into FY2026/27, alongside residual risk from any resumption of Red Sea security incidents.

By the Numbers

  • $4.67bn — Suez Canal FY2025/26 revenue, +23% YoY
  • 1.0m TEU — DP World Sokhna's record 2025 throughput
  • 1.75m TEU — Sokhna's combined basin capacity
  • 34% — CMA CGM's YoY increase in Suez tonnage
  • ~50% — Canal receipts vs. the FY2022/23 pre-crisis peak
  • $7.3bn — New FY2025/26 investment into SCZone (117 projects)

Global Container Ports Post Mixed H1 2026

Major European gateways stagnated in H1 2026 — Rotterdam (7.02m TEU, -0.1%) and Antwerp-Bruges (6.8m TEU, -1.5%) were flat to down — while smaller emerging hubs outgrew them sharply: Cebu (+7.7%), Salerno (+6.2%) and Aqaba (+1%) all posted gains. Houston grew 3% in North America. The divergence suggests trade is diffusing toward secondary and regional hubs rather than concentrating in legacy gateways — relevant context for how Sokhna's East-West positioning competes for diverted volumes.

Source: Container News

Sector

SCZone Courts US Port-Technology Partners

Egyptian and US officials met on August 24 to explore maritime infrastructure, cybersecurity and port-automation partnerships within the Suez Canal Economic Zone, alongside a broader push to localise 21 industrial sectors. East Port Said was cited as the top-ranked container port in Africa/Middle East on the World Bank's 2024 Container Port Performance Index — a useful benchmark for Sokhna's competitive set.

Source: Daily News Egypt

Portfolio Company — DP World Sokhna

Record 2025 Throughput Confirmed; Logistics Park Fully Operational

DP World Sokhna closed 2025 with record annual throughput of 1 million TEU across its three basins (1.75 million TEU combined capacity). The adjoining $85m Sokhna Logistics Park — Egypt's first fully integrated logistics distribution hub, roughly 10km from the port — is now live with bonded warehousing, customs clearance and container handling under one roof, and has since added new service agreements and handled its first passenger-vehicle export shipment. Combined with the Suez traffic recovery above, the asset is well placed heading into the next reporting period.

Sources: WorldCargo News · DP World · Egypt Today

Power, Energy & MineralsSector Watch

Portfolio Company — Globeleq

Cameroon Government Moves to Take Control of Two Globeleq-Majority Plants

Cameroon's government is negotiating an estimated CFA80bn (~$135m) buyout of Globeleq's 56% stakes in the Kribi (216MW gas-fired) and Dibamba (88MW heavy fuel oil) plants, together over a fifth of capacity on the Southern Interconnected Grid. The move follows the state exercising a pre-emptive right in July 2025; talks were reported as ongoing on August 10 with a target close before year-end. State distributor Socadel currently pays close to CFA8bn/month for this power — the government projects CFA36bn in annual savings post-acquisition. This is a live divestment risk/opportunity for the I&C book worth flagging to the valuation and portfolio teams directly.

Source: Business in Cameroon

Portfolio Company — Globeleq

Zambia Build-Out Continues: $315m Solar-Battery Plant, Lunsemfwa Hydro

Elsewhere in the portfolio, Globeleq is pressing ahead with diversification: a confirmed majority-stake acquisition in Zambia's Lunsemfwa Hydro Power Company, a $315m solar-plus-battery storage project (Bloomberg, April 2026), and continued advancement of a third 35MW geothermal unit at Menengai, Kenya. Zambia is described internally as a "priority market."

Sources: Bloomberg · African Energy · ThinkGeoEnergy

Ghana Manganese Targets Record 700,000t in August

Ghana Manganese Company — sole producer/exporter of the mineral nationally, targeting 8m tonnes/year from its Nsuta open-pit mine — guided to a record 700,000t for August 2026 on improved weather and newly commissioned equipment, with over 70% of output already destined for the EU. A reminder of the export-logistics demand feeding West/Central African port capacity generally.

Sources: Citi Newsroom · Rio Times

Sector

Africa's Critical-Minerals Position Still Dominant, Still Contested

Africa holds outsized reserve shares across platinum group metals (79.3%), cobalt (54.5%), manganese (36.5%) and bauxite (25.5%), but faces headwinds: a 6.4% platinum production decline forecast for 2025 (South African weather/restructuring) and a 50% US tariff on copper imports effective August 2025. Guinea's Simandou iron-ore megaproject ($26bn+ invested) remains the region's flagship infrastructure-linked mining story.

Source: Mining Technology
