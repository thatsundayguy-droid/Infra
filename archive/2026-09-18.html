Sanaa / Bab el-Mandeb · Trade & Logistics

Bab el-Mandeb Traffic Collapses 88% as Hormuz Also Grinds to a Halt — Both Chokepoints Now Near-Zero at Once

Shipping through the Bab el-Mandeb Strait came to a near-standstill this week: just four vessels transited in the 24 hours to 17 September, against a daily average of 33 — an 88% drop, per IMF PortWatch data cited by Anadolu. Oil and chemical tanker passage fell 91%, with only one tanker making the crossing; container ship traffic was down 86%. The few vessels that did transit flew the flags of Panama, Singapore, Liberia and Ethiopia. The collapse follows the Houthis' capture of Mokha, the Hanish archipelago, Mayun (Perim) island and Dhubab over the past week, giving the group control of the Yemeni side of the strait for the first time.

The Strait of Hormuz shows a similar picture: shipping data reviewed by RFE/RL put recent Hormuz crossings at just four vessels in a 24-hour period, against a pre-war baseline of roughly 125 vessels a day. For the first time since this crisis began in February, both of the Middle East's major chokepoints are reporting traffic near zero simultaneously. Bab el-Mandeb normally carries an estimated 10% of global maritime trade and 12% of seaborne crude; with Saudi Arabia's Red Sea alternative to Hormuz now itself effectively blocked, pressure shifts further onto the Cape of Good Hope route and, for Mediterranean-origin cargo not dependent on Bab el-Mandeb, onto Suez traffic from the north. We could not confirm a Suez-specific transit count for this exact week as of publication — a priority check for next edition.

This Quarter in the I&C BookQ3 2026 Recap · 1 Jul–18 Sep

This week marks the sharpest point yet in the chokepoint crisis that has defined Q3: both Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb are now reporting traffic near zero at the same time, a first since the crisis began in February. That geopolitical backdrop continues to dominate the ports bucket; on the company side, Globeleq's Cameroon exit remains the quarter's clearest confirmed divestment, and Sokhna's logistics park phase-two tenant signings (corrected into our record in Edition No. 4) are the clearest positive company-specific data point.

Exits & New Investments This Quarter
Exit in progress — Globeleq (Cameroon)

Unchanged this period. Globeleq has been working to divest its 56% stakes in Kribi Power Development Company and Dibamba Power Development Company since at least October 2025 — first via international buyers, now via a CFA80bn (~$135m) government buyout under negotiation since the state exercised a pre-emptive right in July 2025. A June 2026 fiscal dispute (account freeze, grid-capacity withdrawal affecting ~40% of Littoral/West consumers) added urgency. No formal offer had been transmitted as of the most recent reporting; a close before year-end remains the stated expectation.

Adjacent — BII–Africa50 Infrastructure Acceleration Fund

Unchanged this period. BII's $20m commitment to the Africa50 Infrastructure Acceleration Fund (11 August) took the fund to a fourth close of ~$330m; its priority sectors overlap directly with this book and could be a source of future co-investment or pipeline referrals.

No other confirmed or reported exits or new investments were found this quarter for DP World Sokhna, DP World ENAF, AWID, H1 Capital, or GSEZ Minerals.
