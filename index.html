Both Chokepoints Squeezed at Once: Qatar Warns on Bab el-Mandeb as Hormuz Shows a Partial Pulse

Qatar's foreign ministry warned on 15 September that losing access to Bab el-Mandeb alongside the already-blocked Strait of Hormuz would be "catastrophic" for global trade, days after Houthi forces seized Yemen's Mokha port and Perim Island (covered in yesterday's edition). Fresh weekly data shows the two chokepoints moving in different directions at once: Bab el-Mandeb traffic is currently running at roughly 2.2 times Hormuz's volume, according to MarineTraffic, even as Hormuz itself posted a modest rebound — 118 crossings for the week, up 53.2% from 77 the week before, though still far below the pre-crisis baseline of roughly 85 transits a day.

Oil markets are pricing the compounded risk: Brent crude touched its highest level since late May this week, trading around $104 a barrel. Bab el-Mandeb has absorbed much of the rerouted Saudi crude since Hormuz's closure — U.S. EIA data shows oil and product flows through the strait's southern entrance climbing from 5.4 million barrels a day in Q4 2025 to 8.1 million a day in Q2 2026. For DP World Sokhna, the read-through is unchanged in direction but sharper in stakes: continued Suez traffic remains the base case, but the margin for error around both flanking chokepoints has narrowed further this week.

By the Numbers

  • 2.2x — Bab el-Mandeb's current traffic vs. Hormuz (MarineTraffic)
  • 118 — Hormuz weekly crossings, up 53.2% from 77 the week before
  • ~85/day — pre-crisis Hormuz baseline, still far off
  • $104 — Brent crude, highest since late May
  • 5.4m → 8.1m bbl/d — oil/product flow via Bab el-Mandeb's southern entrance, Q4 2025 to Q2 2026 (EIA)

Correction: Logistics Park's Second Phase Appears to Have Already Launched

Updates our "unconfirmed" note in Editions No. 2–3.

Further digging turned up a 1 July 2026 report (the first day of this quarter) describing DP World's launch of "its first integrated logistics distribution hub" at the Sokhna Logistics Park, at its full ~300,000 sqm footprint, with three newly signed tenant agreements: KTDA DMMC (the trading arm of Kenya Tea Development Company), Premium-Line Middle East & Africa (regional arm of the German network-infrastructure manufacturer), and Middle East Logistics Services, a consumer-goods distributor. This matches the previously guided Q3 2026 timeline for phase two and should be treated as the more current status than our earlier "unconfirmed" framing. The same report also cited a 35% year-on-year surge in Sokhna's Q1 2026 transit trade volume.

Exits & New Investments This Quarter
Exit in progress — Globeleq (Cameroon)

Unchanged this period. Globeleq has been working to divest its 56% stakes in Kribi Power Development Company and Dibamba Power Development Company since at least October 2025 — first via international buyers, now via a CFA80bn (~$135m) government buyout under negotiation since the state exercised a pre-emptive right in July 2025. A June 2026 fiscal dispute (account freeze, grid-capacity withdrawal affecting ~40% of Littoral/West consumers) added urgency. No formal offer had been transmitted as of the most recent reporting; a close before year-end remains the stated expectation.

Adjacent — BII–Africa50 Infrastructure Acceleration Fund

Unchanged this period. BII's $20m commitment to the Africa50 Infrastructure Acceleration Fund (11 August) took the fund to a fourth close of ~$330m; its priority sectors overlap directly with this book and could be a source of future co-investment or pipeline referrals.

No other confirmed or reported exits or new investments were found this quarter for DP World Sokhna, DP World ENAF, AWID, H1 Capital, or GSEZ Minerals.
