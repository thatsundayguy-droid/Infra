Suez Canal Revival Accelerates as Hormuz Crisis Reroutes Global Shipping — Sokhna's Tailwind Builds

Suez Canal revenue climbed 42% year-on-year to $505m in July 2026 — the highest monthly total since December 2023 — as the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz under the ongoing Iran war, together with intermittent Houthi threats in the southern Red Sea, pushed more shipping onto the Egyptian route. A total of 1,340 vessels transited the canal that month, according to state statistics agency CAPMAS, up 27% on July 2025 and above June's 1,208 crossings. Oil tankers accounted for 526 of July's transits, versus 485 in June — a shift analysts link in part to the rerouting of Saudi Arabian oil exports away from Hormuz, which has been effectively shut to shipping since a US-Israeli campaign against Iran began in late February.

For the I&C book, the read-through builds on last edition's: DP World Sokhna sits at the mouth of this recovery, having closed 2025 at a record 1 million TEU against 1.75 million TEU of combined basin capacity. The Suez Canal Authority now expects full-year 2026 revenue of $5.8bn–$6bn, up from $4.1bn in 2025, though both traffic and revenue remain well below the pre-crisis 2023 benchmark. No Sokhna-specific operational news was found this period, but continued canal traffic gains — plus the logistics park's second phase, still slated for this quarter — bear watching into the next edition.

Sector

Hormuz Disruption Enters Its Seventh Month

The Strait of Hormuz has been effectively closed to normal shipping since 28 February 2026, when a US-Israeli campaign against Iran began; the strait typically carries around a quarter of the world's seaborne oil trade and a fifth of its LNG. A ceasefire framework from April largely held until early July, when fighting resumed and both sides reportedly declared the earlier memorandum void. As of this writing, the strait's status remains unresolved, and the rerouting described in the lead story is expected to persist into the autumn.

Sources: Transport Topics / Bloomberg · Strait of Hormuz crisis timeline

This Quarter in the I&C BookQ3 2026 Recap · 1 Jul–14 Sep

The dominant story of the quarter has been geopolitical rather than company-specific: the Strait of Hormuz's effective closure since late February, and the resulting rerouting of oil and container traffic toward the Suez Canal, reshaped the trading backdrop for the entire ports bucket and, by extension, DP World Sokhna. Company-level newsflow across the other five names was comparatively quiet — a reminder that disclosure cadence itself is a data point worth tracking. A condensed recap of each name follows.

DP World Sokhna

Closed 2025 at a record 1m TEU against 1.75m TEU of capacity. Benefited through Q3 from the Hormuz-driven Suez traffic recovery (canal revenue +42% YoY in July). The Sokhna Logistics Park's second phase remained guided for Q3 2026 completion throughout the quarter, unconfirmed as of 14 September.

DP World ENAF

No distinct operational news disclosed at any point during Q3.

AWID

No new mandates disclosed beyond the Naivasha SEZ bulk water project, whose PIP approval predates this quarter. BII's broader Africa50 MoU (11 August) named water and sanitation a priority vertical for adjacent capital.

Globeleq

Two threads ran through the quarter: (1) Cameroon's government continued negotiating an estimated CFA80bn (~$135m) buyout of Globeleq's 56% stakes in the Kribi and Dibamba plants, following its July 2025 pre-emption right — still unresolved as of 14 September, with no formal offer reported transmitted; and (2) continued Zambia build-out, including the completed Lunsemfwa Hydro majority-stake acquisition, the $315m solar-battery project, and advancement of a third geothermal unit at Menengai, Kenya. Zambia hosts the Africa Critical Minerals Conference on 15–16 September, just after this recap's window.

H1 Capital

No distinct news disclosed during Q3. BII's public portfolio page shows one active and one exited investment in the company.

GSEZ Minerals

No distinct news disclosed during Q3. The Gabon Mineral Port's capacity has more than tripled from its original 2m-tonne design to roughly 7m tonnes and is reported operating near full capacity, per the most recent public data available (DFC).
