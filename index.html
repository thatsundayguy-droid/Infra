Sanaa / Suez · Trade & Logistics

Bab el-Mandeb Risk Resurfaces as Houthis Seize Mokha and Perim Island — Both Red Sea Chokepoints Now in Play

Yemen's Houthi rebels captured the Red Sea port city of Mokha on 10 September and the nearby Perim (Mayun) island the following day, extending their control over the Bab el-Mandeb Strait — the very route much of the Saudi oil rerouted away from the closed Strait of Hormuz has been relying on. Ship crossings through Bab el-Mandeb fell sharply in the hours after the capture, from 30 vessels on Wednesday to 12 by Thursday evening, with only one of those twelve entering the Red Sea versus 17 the day before, according to preliminary Kpler data. Saudi Arabia struck Mokha's airport in response; Houthi forces also fired missiles and drones at a Saudi military airbase this week, and a planned Monday meeting in Oman to discuss reopening Hormuz was postponed at Saudi Arabia's request.

For the I&C book, the immediate risk is to the Suez recovery flagged in last edition's lead story: for the first time this year, both of the Middle East's major chokepoints — Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb — carry meaningful disruption risk at once. So far the canal itself has proven resilient: weekly transits rose to 256 for 7–13 September (+3.2% week-on-week), and Cosco and OOCL are resuming five combined Suez services this week despite the tension, with Linerlytica projecting 27 major-carrier transits for 13–20 September. But daily Suez traffic reportedly dipped from 42 crossings on Thursday to 24 on Friday immediately after the Mokha news, before recovering to a weekly high of 43 on Sunday — a reminder that the Sokhna tailwind sits on genuinely volatile ground.

Power, Energy & MineralsSector Watch

Portfolio Company — Globeleq

Cameroon: A Confirmed Exit Process, Not Just a Negotiation

Additional background surfaced this period sharpens the picture of the Kribi/Dibamba situation flagged in prior editions. Globeleq has reportedly been trying to exit Cameroon since at least October 2025, first pursuing international buyers — talks with Saudi Arabia's Aqua Power for a majority stake reportedly did not materialise — before pivoting to local players. A separate fiscal dispute escalated in June 2026: Cameroonian authorities froze Globeleq's bank accounts, and Globeleq responded by withdrawing generating capacity from the grid, reportedly cutting power to roughly 40% of consumers in the Littoral and West regions. The government's CFA80bn (~$135m) buyout of Globeleq's 56% stakes in Kribi and Dibamba remains the most likely resolution path, with talks still described as progressing toward a possible year-end close and no formal offer yet transmitted.

This Quarter in the I&C BookQ3 2026 Recap · 1 Jul–16 Sep

The dominant story of the quarter remains geopolitical: the Strait of Hormuz's effective closure since late February, joined this week by fresh Houthi gains around Bab el-Mandeb, has kept the ports bucket volatile and shaped the trading backdrop for DP World Sokhna throughout. Company-level newsflow across the other five names stayed quiet, with one exception — Globeleq's Cameroon exit, now tracked below as this quarter's most advanced portfolio-level divestment.

Exits & New Investments This Quarter
Exit in progress — Globeleq (Cameroon)

The clearest exit signal in the book. Globeleq has been working to divest its 56% stakes in Kribi Power Development Company and Dibamba Power Development Company since at least October 2025 — first via international buyers, now via a CFA80bn (~$135m) government buyout under negotiation since the state exercised a pre-emptive right in July 2025. A June 2026 fiscal dispute (account freeze, grid-capacity withdrawal) has added urgency. No formal offer had been transmitted as of the most recent reporting; a close before year-end remains the stated expectation.

Adjacent — BII–Africa50 Infrastructure Acceleration Fund

Not a direct stake in a named portfolio company, but the quarter's clearest new capital commitment touching this book: BII's $20m commitment to the Africa50 Infrastructure Acceleration Fund (11 August), taking the fund to a fourth close of ~$330m. The fund's priority sectors — power, water, transport/logistics — overlap directly with the I&C book and could be a source of future co-investment or pipeline referrals.

No other confirmed or reported exits or new investments were found this quarter for DP World Sokhna, DP World ENAF, AWID, H1 Capital, or GSEZ Minerals.
